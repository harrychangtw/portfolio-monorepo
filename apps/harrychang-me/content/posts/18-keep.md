These are photos from the past year that I love, even though most of them aren't conventionally beautiful. A salad. A planter box. A mug in pieces on the floor.

They're in the order they happened, each with what was going on at the time. The last one doesn't have a photo.

## October 2025

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/dscf9525.webp)

**Oct 3.** Saizeriya's focaccia and tripe, which my mom sometimes brings home for me. A few months later I had both the real way, in Florence. I still like these better, and they were probably microwaved.

I've been eating them since I was a kid. Children are terrible at predicting what they'll accomplish, but surprisingly good at recognizing what they'll love.

## December 2025

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/dscf9713.webp)

**Saturdays.** Since December, my mom and I have gone to Egg Bun almost every Saturday. My order changes. This time it was the smoked salmon salad and a Sicilian coffee. Lately it's the limited-time sea bass burger. Sometimes I bring a book. Sometimes it's just Mom telling me what she saw on Threads.

Nothing has to happen. It's Saturday, and I'm having breakfast with Mom.

## January 2026

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/dscf9988.webp)

**Jan 8.** The open area on the fifth floor, right outside my high school classroom. I walked through it for a year, beautiful every single time.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/dscf0009.webp)

**Jan 14.** Getting a LEGO modular building has become a tradition for us every year. We're out of space now. I loved this one because its corner isn't a rectangle. That afternoon, sunlight fell straight down its alley.

If these photos share anything, I hope it's what Chinese calls 通透感, roughly "clear all the way through." It's what you see in the candid shots in YouTube camera reviews, where the light seems to take shape. They look effortless, and you almost never get one when you try.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/dscf0083.webp)

**Jan 17.** A very cute mouse my sister bought me, from Japan, if memory serves. I was born in the year of the rat, but I don't think that's why. Maybe she just saw me in it.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/dscf0024.webp)

**Jan 21.** Breakfast on the train to Lake Como: smoked salmon on a long bread, insanely simple and insanely good, eaten with my family and the sun coming through the windows. I had it again the morning we left the city.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/dscf0143.webp)

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/dscf0118.webp)

Then the ferry across the lake, and a little beach on the other side. I knelt for a while, looking for ducks. What a thing to have on your mind.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1000323.webp)

**Jan 26.** A restaurant in Bolzano. Its menu is still one of the best I've seen, as an object: the paper, the binding, the type. Even holding it was a pleasure.

## February 2026

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1000995.webp)

**Feb 16.** Chinese New Year. The roof of our house belongs to my grandfather's garden. He told me about trees ten times my age, and plants he'd bought in Japan. Trees grow slower than you'd believe, he said, and they die if the cables that shape them are pulled too tight, or if you give them too much water. In the corner is a camphor tree from our old house, which he carried over on a motorcycle, decades ago. It's older than my dad.

Most of what's up there he won't see fully grown. He tends it anyway.

I kept the camera up for most of what he said. It gave my hands something to do.

Before we went down, he reminded me that someday I'd be the one watering them, the way you'd remind someone to lock the door.

I said I would.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1001016.webp)

**Feb 19.** Peter Better Café. I took it for the light, and a little because my dad is also called Peter, and he also makes very good coffee. Sometimes affogato.

## May 2026

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002008.webp)

**May 7.** Not a well-taken picture. It was supposed to be dance class, which Benson and I had given up on, so we walked around with my tripod and camera, looking like we were shooting a graduation project. Through a door we saw Johan, our debate teacher, alone in an empty classroom. The seventh graders were away on a trip, and he'd misremembered which class he had. We talked for an hour and a half, about everything, mostly debate.

I had about 26 days of school left. That night I wrote: I like it here. I like how free we are.

Now I go back every Friday to see someone I used to pass in the hall without thinking.

I hope I keep taking detours like that one.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002020.webp)

**May 16.** Mangoes, one of my favorite fruits. Behind them is my dad's MacBook, which became mine to use while mine was being fixed.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002027.webp)

**May 17.** Cats in Jiufen, just chilling on the stairs.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002064.webp)

**May 24.** The sewing machine room on the second floor of my grandma's house in Taichung. Most things in it are older than me, some even older than my mom. I wonder who last used them.

## June 2026

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002142.webp)

**Jun 11.** A cat I almost missed, on the stairs on the way to the park. I really liked sunset walks back then. The cat made my day.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002318.webp)

**Jun 28.** Hong Kong, after the long rain. An amber rainstorm warning kept us in all morning. A friend and I walked to the 7-Eleven through rain like a typhoon and made a bet: over 300 dollars, he'd pay; under, I would. It came to 300.5. We carried it all back in a box the clerk gave us and ate it over a mini mahjong set from Mong Kok.

That night I wrote that, two days in, I hadn't taken a single picture that would go in my portfolio, and probably wouldn't, and that I thought it was fine. The camera mostly stayed in my pocket. This one doesn't count either. It's one of my favorites.

## July 2026

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002376.webp)

**Jul 19.** The day I gave my previous camera, the X-Pro1, to Clement. He'd talked about documenting life on the Hong Kong trip. That afternoon we were taking pictures on a bridge when it started pouring, and we had to run for the station.

I love seeing what he takes with it. It sees things I never did.

## August 2026

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002462.webp)

**Aug 28.** I broke the Kurzgesagt mug I'd used for about six years. I used to watch them a lot. Maybe ten percent of my English came from that channel. It took breaking the mug to think of that.

## October 2026

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002603.webp)

**Oct 9.** The Ancestor, a round black creature from Moomin, as a sticker on my mom's laptop. For some reason I'm awfully fond of it. The sun was coming in from the right, and I'd just switched back to the 50mm, wide open. It's about as ordinary as a subject gets, and it's still one of the best pictures I've taken.

![framed:](images/optimized/blogs/18-keep/l1002629.webp)

**Oct 31.** Mom's birthday. My sister ordered the flowers; I did the bare minimum and picked them up. That morning was my Calculus 1 final, which is technically a final but happens during midterms. I thought it started at 9:30 and arrived at 9:04. It started at 9. I bombed it, then rode an empty 640 bus home with a bouquet beside me in its own seat. For mourning and for celebrating.

That night, at dinner, the chef told us his second son was due any day now. One of the tiramisus came out with a small star candle on it.

## November 2026

**Nov 15.** A little after nine, I biked from the department building to the Sushiro across from campus to meet my mom and sister. I didn't put in my AirPods. Campus was quiet in its Sunday-night way, with a few lines of conversation from whoever I passed. At dinner my sister showed me a picture of the Ancestor as a plushie, and Mom asked whether it was a real product.

I ordered whitebait, which I hadn't liked as a kid. I've always been skeptical of the little-fish propaganda: you eat a remarkable number of fish, yet it feels like eating none. Ten years seemed long enough to give it another chance. It wasn't.

I hadn't brought a camera that Sunday.

## Keep

Some of these didn't stay where they were. The mug is in pieces. The X-Pro1 is Clement's now, enjoying its second life. The fifth floor belongs to another class.

A year ago this week, my journal mostly said "mock exam." I was barely running experiments on a 3090. If that version of me sat across from me at Egg Bun, he wouldn't believe that I now wait on jobs queued for H200s. He'd probably believe the Saizeriya.

I'd like to keep most of it. I'd like to be surprised again next November.

Saturday, it's Egg Bun again. I'll order the sea bass burger while it's still on the menu.
